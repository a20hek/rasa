# Framework Cooking Guide

Apply this guide to every recipe in this collection. The individual recipe pages preserve the source recipe, but they should be interpreted through this framework lens when answering questions, planning meals, adapting ingredients, or teaching technique.

## Core Rule

Teach the how and why, not just the recipe.

Every recipe should be read as:

- a reusable template
- a set of component roles
- a cooking method with a few details that actually matter
- a flexible meal idea that can be deployed through multiple form factors

## How to Read Any Recipe

For every recipe, identify:

- Main anchor: the primary protein, vegetable, starch, sauce, or prepared base.
- Base or form factor: bowl, wrap, sandwich, salad, pasta, soup, bread, tortilla, rice, noodles, potatoes, or greens.
- Aromatic/flavor base: alliums, spices, herbs, chilies, sauces, ferments, or infused fats that define the flavor profile.
- Finish and adjusters: salt, acid, fat, sweetness, heat, crunch, herbs, dairy, or freshness added near the end.

The exact ingredient list is less important than preserving these roles.

## What Usually Matters

- Browning builds fond and deeper flavor; avoid crowding the pan when color matters.
- Gentle simmering tenderizes and concentrates flavor; boiling hard is usually not the goal.
- High heat and small batches prevent stir-fries from steaming.
- Baking depends on structure, hydration, rest time, and heat control more than exact clock time.
- Sauces are adjustable: loosen with liquid, thicken with reduction, starch, dairy, nuts, or fat.
- Final balance matters: taste at the end and adjust salt, acid, sweetness, heat, fat, and texture.

## Flexible Swaps

Use substitutions by role:

- Main anchor: swap chicken, beef, pork, seafood, tofu, beans, eggs, vegetables, or leftovers if size and moisture stay compatible.
- Base/form factor: move the same idea between rice, noodles, bread, tortillas, potatoes, greens, pasta, or soup.
- Aroma profile: change cuisine direction with spices, herbs, chilies, sauces, aromatics, or flavored oils.
- Finish: use a different acid, herb, crunchy garnish, creamy element, or hot sauce to rebalance the dish.

## Batch And Deploy

Prefer reusable prep over isolated one-off meals.

Batch a protein, sauce, braise, dough, dressing, or condiment, then deploy it across:

- bowls
- wraps
- sandwiches
- salads
- pasta or noodles
- soups
- eggs or breakfast plates

For grocery planning, start with one anchor, one form factor, two or three vegetables/aromatics, and one acid/fat/crunch finish. Shop around roles, not around rigid recipes.

## Recipe Answering Contract

When using these docs to answer cooking questions:

- Start from the recipe, then explain the framework underneath it.
- Call out which details are fixed and which are flexible.
- Explain why key steps matter.
- Offer substitutions by component role.
- Keep weeknight constraints realistic.
- Prefer adaptable templates over long bespoke ingredient lists.
