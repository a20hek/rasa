# Bloom

Source path: `/fundamental/technique/bloom`

Toasting aromatics to release their flavors, usually in hot oil.

## What are the fundamentals of blooming?

Blooming is the act of toasting aromatics to release their flavors, usually in hot oil.

- Fats and oils are effective solvents. They release fat-soluble flavor compounds from aromatics and spices that cannot be unlocked by water alone. Heat helps this process happen rapidly.
- Fat holds onto aromatic compounds better than water, infusing the bloomed flavors throughout a dish.

Blooming ingredients can develop toasted flavor compounds via the [Maillard Reaction](../reactions/maillard.md), although high heat can quickly burn them, resulting in a bitter taste.

In some cases, like saffron in a Paella, delicate spices or herbs can be "bloomed" in warm water or broth, although that is more of an infusion, like making tea or stock.

> Kitchen Tool: Pan

> Heat Type: Conduction w/ fat

> Pan Surface Temperature: 300°F/148°C+

> Food Reaction(s): [Maillard](../reactions/maillard.md)

### Example Foods

- Tadka
- Refried beans
- Paella
