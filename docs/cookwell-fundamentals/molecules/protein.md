# Protein

Source path: `/fundamental/molecule/protein`

A building block of many foods & essential for certain cooking reactions. Sensitive to heat, salt, and acid.

## What are the fundamentals of protein?

Protein is an essential building block of food and a life-sustaining molecule.

Out of all basic food molecules, proteins change their structure most easily and dramatically in the presence of heat, salt, or acid, making them especially sensitive to cooking processes.

Usually, protein strands go through denaturation and [Coagulation](../reactions/coagulation.md) during cooking, like an egg white transforming as it heats up. Different protein types such as gluten or collagen can behave differently.

- Gluten protein structures develop into long chains with [Knead](../techniques/knead.md) and vastly change the texture of baked goods.
- Collagen in tough cuts of meat transforms into tender and unctuous gelatin with heat and time, such as in smoked meats.

## How does protein affect the elements of flavor?

Texture - Proteins come in many textures, from crunchy to chewy to tender. Under- or overcooking proteins immensely changes texture. Proteins can even be suspended in liquids, such as in milk.

Physical - Proteins provide long-lasting energy; humans are biologically wired to seek protein-rich foods.

Aroma - Animal proteins can carry aroma molecules that remind us of their signature taste. Most protein flavor is often a result of surrounding fat.

Human - Protein is essential to human life. Different cultures and diets consume different proteins. Protein-rich foods can trigger nostalgia, emotions, or associations.

Taste - Apart from aroma, some proteins are neutral in flavor. When proteins are cooked so Maillard occurs, amino acid structures in protein, such as glutamates, can create umami.

Sight - Protein can show up in most foods and influence the visual element of a dish in infinite ways.
