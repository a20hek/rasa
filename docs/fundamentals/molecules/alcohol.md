# Alcohol

Source path: `/fundamental/molecule/alcohol`

An effective solvent that can be added for flavor or texture purposes in cooking.

## What are the fundamentals of alcohol?

Although alcohol is not a primary nutrient like fat, protein, or carbohydrates, it still provides energy in calories. Alcohol molecules are usually a by-product of [Fermentation](../reactions/fermentation.md).

From a culinary perspective, alcohol can act as an important flavor booster or solvent, and even manipulate texture in food or drink.

- Vanilla extract contains ethanol to fully dissolve and carry vanilla bean aromatics.
- Alcohol is added to batters since it evaporates faster than water when deep fried, leading to crispier foods.

Lastly, alcohol is enjoyed primarily in drinks, for its own flavor or as a food pairing.

### Alcohol-related foods

- Fermented drinks like kombucha or beer
- Vodka sauces
- Beer battered fish
- Red wine pan sauces
- White wine vinegar, apple cider vinegar, etc

## How does alcohol affect the elements of flavor?

Aroma - Because of solvent properties, alcohol releases complex aroma molecules in other ingredients. In vodka sauce, ethanol releases additional fruity esters in tomatoes. Alcohol can add fermented or malted aromas, or distinct infused flavors like juniper berries in gin.

Texture - In baked goods or batters, alcohol can make for a crispier or lighter texture because ethanol evaporates more readily than water. In mixed drinks, spirits have a different viscosity and mouthfeel than water.

Taste - Alcohol molecules, most commonly ethanol, taste slightly sweet and bitter by themselves. Usually, these tastes are overwhelmed by aromas of what the alcohol is made of.

Physical - Alcohol has a psychological effect on the brain, changing perceptions of food and surroundings. High-proof alcohol burns when sipped because it increases temperature sensitivity of mouth pain receptors.

Human - Alcohol or drinks can change the experience or ambiance of a meal. Different cultures have different traditions or associations with alcohol and food.

Sight - Alcohol can change dish color, like red wine braised short ribs. Cocktail presentation, packaging, or glassware can change perception too.
