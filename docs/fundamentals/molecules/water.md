# Water

Source path: `/fundamental/molecule/water`

An effective thermal regulator, heat conductor, and source of moisture.

## What are the fundamentals of water?

Water is present in most whole ingredients, and is an essential building block of organic matter, although it does not provide energy in calories.

- Water is effective at transferring and regulating heat, making it a popular thermal medium for cooking in many techniques.
- Water is part of most natural ingredients and often makes up a large percentage of their weight, such as in meats, vegetables, seeds, nuts, and grains.

Exceptions like pressed oils, rendered fats, dehydrated spices or grains, & milled flours will not contain water content.

- Water is crucial for life to exist but also allows bacterial growth in foods. For this reason, many foods are dehydrated to extend shelf life.

## How does water affect the elements of flavor?

Texture - Water's ability to exist in all physical forms means dishes depend on water for texture. Ice cream needs water to freeze. A sauce needs water to flow. A poached egg requires simmering water, and steamed vegetables depend on water vapor.

Physical - Humans need water to survive. When foods are devoid of water, they taste dry and unappetizing or are hard to eat, chew, and swallow.

Human - Water allows many human experiences tied to food. A sizzling platter of fajitas is not possible without water vapor. A refreshing balanced cocktail is not possible without enough water dilution.

Sight - Water is tied to color or visual appeal. A steak that has not seared properly because of excess moisture may appear grey, while blanched asparagus is more green and vibrant thanks to its water bath.

Taste - Water on its own has no taste but is a great solvent of other ingredients. It can carry water-soluble flavor compounds that register as any of the five tastes.

Aroma - Water has no aroma on its own, but steam can carry volatile aromas, and water can be infused with soluble flavor compounds.
