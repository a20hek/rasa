# Steam

Source path: `/fundamental/technique/steam`

Cooking with water vapor.

## What are the fundamentals of steaming?

Steaming is cooking with vaporized water, which happens at exactly 212°F/100°C at sea level.

This lower temperature is particularly suitable for delicate foods like vegetables, fish, poultry, and dumplings, resulting in moist and tender dishes with a fresh and vibrant taste.

- It requires no fat to cook, although it can be used in conjunction with fats in a pan to crisp the bottom of ingredients and keep them from sticking.

Steam is a more efficient cooking method than baking or roasting because it transfers heat faster than air at a similar temperature.

> Kitchen Tool: Steaming baskets, covered pan, parchment packets

> Heat Type: Convection w/ vaporized air & water

> Temperature: 212°F/100°C

> Food Reaction(s): [Coagulation](../reactions/coagulation.md), [Gelation](../reactions/gelation.md)

### Example Foods

- Gyoza
- Steamed vegetables
- Fish en papillote
