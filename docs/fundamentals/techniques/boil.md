# Boil

Source path: `/fundamental/technique/boil`

Cooking food in 100°C bubbling water.

## What are the fundamentals of boiling?

Boiling happens when water is heated to a temperature where rolling bubbles begin to happen, which is 100°C at sea level.

Because water transfers heat faster than air, boiled items will cook through faster than if baked or roasted at similar temperatures.

- Boiling is similar to [Deep Fry](./deep-fry.md) in that it submerges food in a hot liquid, except it uses [Water](../molecules/water.md) instead of [Fat](../molecules/fat.md) as the thermal medium.
- Food in boiling water will never rise above 100°C, and thus it will never reach the required temperatures for the [Maillard Reaction](../reactions/maillard.md).

> Kitchen Tool: Pot

> Heat Type: Water Conduction

> Water Temperature: 100°C

> Food Reaction(s): [Gelation](../reactions/gelation.md), [Coagulation](../reactions/coagulation.md)

### Example foods

- Boiling pasta
- Hard-boiled eggs
- Boiled carrots
