# Caramelization

Source path: `/fundamental/reaction/caramelization`

Browning of sugar molecules that changes their flavors, color, and texture.

## What are the fundamentals of caramelization?

Caramelization is a culinary and chemical process that occurs when sugars are heated to a certain temperature where they undergo a series of complex chemical reactions. These reactions result in the characteristic golden-brown color and rich flavor associated with caramelized foods.

When exposed to relatively high heat, sugars begin to melt and darken in color. Pure sugar melts around 320°F/160°C and begins rapidly caramelizing around 338°F/170°C and upward. From here the color darkens, new flavors develop, and depending on final caramelized temperature and added ingredients, the mixture can crystalize into different textures.

This same process happens in sugars found in fruits and vegetable ingredients, which can lead to lightly crispy or browned exteriors when cooked.

- Unlike the [Maillard Reaction](./maillard.md), which requires both protein amino acids and sugars, caramelization can happen in the presence of specific [Carbohydrates](../molecules/carbohydrate.md) alone.

> Catalyst: Heat

> Relevant techniques: [Bake](../techniques/bake.md), [Saute](../techniques/saute.md), [Simmer](../techniques/simmer.md)

> Relevant molecules: [Carbohydrates](../molecules/carbohydrate.md)

### Example foods

- Creme brulee
- Caramelized onions
- Dulce de leche
- Roasted carrots & beet

## How does caramelization affect the elements of flavor?

In order of importance:

Aroma - Caramelization creates hundreds of new aroma molecules, leading to complex flavors that are not anything like raw counterparts.

Texture - Caramelization can turn dry sugars into liquids, chewy caramels, or hard crystalized textures. Because caramelization usually happens at temperatures that evaporate water and dehydrate the surface of foods, it can result in crisp exterior textures.

Taste - While caramelization mainly creates new aromas, the reaction accentuates sweetness by changing sugar properties in certain fruits or starchy vegetables. Taken too far, browning can turn some ingredients bitter.

Sight - Because you can control caramelization with temperature and time, you can dial in the color of a dish to desired browning levels.

Human - Humans associate "caramel" with a certain color and taste. Often caramelized sweet food can have strong emotional triggers, evoke childhood memories, or be associated with a special occasion or tradition.

Physical - The sight and complex aromas of a well-caramelized food may trigger salivation.
