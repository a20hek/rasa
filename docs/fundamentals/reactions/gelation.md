# Gelation

Source path: `/fundamental/reaction/gelation`

The swelling of starch molecules when hydrated and heated.

## What are the fundamentals of gelation?

When starches, a [Carbohydrate](../molecules/carbohydrate.md), are combined with a liquid and heated, they absorb moisture and begin to swell in a process known as gelation. Starches begin gelating at different temperatures depending on their gelation range.

In a stir fry or pan sauce, a cornstarch slurry is added, which gelates and thickens the sauce as it simmers. In other cases, ingredients might already contain naturally gelling properties.

- Some fruits contain pectin, a special kind of starch adept at thickening macerations into a jam.
- Sliced potatoes have lots of loose surface starch, which gelates if boiled, and can be set into a crispy exterior if dehydrated and fried.
- Short-grain rice releases starch into its cooking liquid, which can gelate during cooking. In risotto or congee, this is encouraged.

Thickened sauces reach peak consistencies at different starch concentrations and temperatures. If simmered or heated too long, swollen starch molecules can break down and thin out the mixture again.

In cooked and dehydrated starch networks, such as bread, starch structures change as they cool and harden. This is retrogradation: over time, cooked, gelated starches reorganize into larger chain structures, resulting in stale & crusty textures.

How is gelation different from [Coagulation](./coagulation.md)? Coagulation happens with proteins, while gelation happens only with carbohydrate starches.

- A custard is set into a solid state through coagulation from protein structure in eggs.
- Using pure gelatin as a thickener creates a gel-like structure through coagulation, since gelatin is a protein, not a starch.

Notes: The terms gelation and gelatinization are often used interchangeably. Cook Well avoids gelatinization because it implies gelatin is involved, which is confusing & inaccurate. A gel is a structure of water and other molecules suspended in a semi-solid state.

- Pasta & dumplings
- Masa corn tortillas & tamales

## How does gelation affect the elements of flavor?

Texture - Gelation primarily affects texture by thickening a liquid or setting cooked starch molecules together.

Human - Different cuisines favor different starch textures, from crispy to chewy, such as rice cakes or mochi compared with al-dente pastas.

Sight - Sauce viscosity or jam jiggle can immediately change how we perceive an eating experience.

Physical - Gelatinized foods do not have a physical effect on the body.

Aroma - Gelation does not affect aroma.

Taste - Gelation does not change food taste.
